Under the hood

How it actually works

The four things worth being able to explain.

Why ICA instead of PCA?

PCA only uses second-order statistics (covariance). It finds uncorrelated directions, but uncorrelated is weaker than independent. Whitened data stays white under any rotation, so covariance cannot tell which rotation recovers the sources. ICA uses higher-order statistics (non-Gaussianity) to choose the right rotation. In step 3, after whitening the cloud is round but still rotated.

What does whitening do, and why does it help?

It rescales and rotates the data, using the eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix, so the covariance becomes the identity. The unknown unmixing matrix is then just a rotation: for 3 sources, 3 free angles instead of 9 free numbers. FastICA only has to search over rotations.

Why does non-Gaussianity separate the sources?

By the Central Limit Theorem, a sum of independent signals is closer to Gaussian than the signals themselves, and each microphone is a weighted sum of the sources. So the most non-Gaussian projection is a single source. You can see this in the "spikiness" numbers: mixtures are lower than the sources, and ICA brings them back. If sources are Gaussian, every rotation looks identical, so ICA tolerates at most one Gaussian source (step 4).

How were the experiments and metrics chosen?

The mix uses a known matrix, so ground truth exists. Outputs come out in arbitrary order, sign and scale, so each is matched to a source by absolute correlation and scored with SI-SDR, which removes the best scale (and sign) before measuring distortion. The best raw microphone is the baseline. The noise and Gaussian tests probe the model's assumptions on purpose.
